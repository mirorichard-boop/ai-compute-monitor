Reading the AI chip cycle: supply, demand, and who absorbs the gap

Two forces set the balance in AI compute. Falling AI prices pull in more usage, and more usage means demand for more chips. New chips, data centers and power connections add supply. Rental prices show which force is ahead. Rising chip rents mean demand is outrunning supply. Falling rents mean supply has caught up. When AI output gets cheaper faster than the chips that produce it, the gap lands on whoever sits between the two.

Prices move before earnings do. This page tracks three of them daily: what it costs to rent an AI chip, what AI output sells for, and how fast Big Tech is spending. Each signal below states, in advance, the rule that would mark a change in direction, so you can check every reading against the numbers.

Seven signals to watch

Each card answers one question. Steady means no turn. Watch means it is moving toward one. Turning means the rule on the card has been met. Signals 1, 2, 3 and 6 track the chip cycle; 7 tracks whether power is the limit. 4 and 5 still need more data.

Terms used

TokenThe unit AI output is sold in, about ¾ of a word. Prices are quoted per million.
Chip rentWhat a company pays per hour to use one AI chip in someone else's data center.
A100 / H100 / H200 / B200Nvidia AI chips, oldest to newest. H100 (2023) is the most widely installed.
CapexCapital spending: data centers, chips, power equipment. Chipmakers' sales follow its growth.
NeocloudA company whose business is renting out AI chips, often bought with borrowed money.
Open modelsAI models anyone can download and run, so many providers sell the identical product on price.
